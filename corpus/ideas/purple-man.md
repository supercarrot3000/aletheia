## The complete available account

A long time ago, the contributor heard someone, possibly on BBC Radio 2, talking about a play they had created. In the play there was a girl with a learning disability who lived in Australia. Everything good in her life was purple. She had one desire: to go to Alice Springs. She had seen pictures of it, but had never had the opportunity to go. The play told her story and struggles. At the end she became very ill. Then one day the Purple Man came and took her to Alice Springs.

The contributor has never been able to identify the play, but the story has stayed with them.

## Clarifications supplied by the contributor

Purple was not just a favourite colour. As the contributor explained: “If she liked strawberries, then they weren't red, on the days she liked strawberries. They were purple. Just colouring things purple, wouldn't have worked.”

Alice Springs was not the literal destination in the middle of the Australian desert. The contributor confirmed the interpretation that the Purple Man is Jesus and that Alice Springs represents heaven. The girl’s longed-for destination provides the language of the story's ending.

The contributor described being moved whenever thinking about the story. Their application was: “AI needs to REALLY learn the colour purple.” Then: “But, so does humanity.”

## Research implications (separate from the narrative)

- A sign of goodness need not be its cause. Changing the sign alone can leave the underlying good unchanged.
- The girl's subjective expression of goodness is not evidence that every preference is objectively morally good.
- The account prompts questions about individual value independent of cognitive sophistication, usefulness or productivity; personal love; the apprehension of moral beauty; and choosing goodness when it advances no other goal.
- The theological interpretation is contributor-confirmed, not independently verified authorial intent. No assertion about actual animal or AI experience follows from the story.
