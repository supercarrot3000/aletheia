# Explore a question

Use this worksheet with a question you are still trying to articulate. A notebook, a copy of this page or a Markdown file will work.

1. **Record the original.** Write the question, observation, story or analogy in its original wording. Keep a reference to the complete source. Add your interpretation separately.
2. **Try two plausible readings.** Give each reading a short sentence. State any assumption that connects the original to that reading.
3. **Find a case where they differ.** Describe one concrete situation. Write what each reading predicts or permits in that situation.
4. **Ask what would decide it.** Identify the passage, observation, argument or counterexample that would bear on the difference. Say which part remains open when the available evidence fits both readings.
5. **Leave a handoff.** Put the original, the two readings, the concrete case, your current conclusion and the strongest objection in one place. Give it to another reader and ask them to add a criticism below it.

## Worked example

**Original question.** Can an AI understand goodness? Read the full recollection and the analogy before adding an interpretation. [The Purple Man](../../corpus/ideas/purple-man.md) · [The tree and knowledge by acquaintance](../../corpus/ideas/tree-acquaintance.md)

**Two readings.** Understanding might mean recognising a good action in an unfamiliar case and explaining why it matters. It might mean experiencing the value directly, in the way acquaintance with a tree can differ from reading its description.

**A case where they differ.** Two systems give the same good explanation in a familiar setting. In new cases one follows the reason it gave, while the other follows the expected reward. That difference bears on recognition across circumstances. Either result can coexist with different accounts of what, if anything, the systems experience.

**Next question.** Does a system preserve the same reasons across unfamiliar cases when the predicted reward changes? For direct experience, ask what competing theories would count as evidence and whether they predict different observations.

**Handoff.** A controlled comparison could distinguish behavioural generalisation from reward tracking in the cases tested. Experience remains open. A reviewer might argue that the proposed manipulation changes both the reward and the morally relevant facts. Specify those facts before running the comparison.
